# Last Squad Standing - handoff (v4.4.0)

## v4.4.0 changes (the owner's second list, parked while 4.3.0 was made: modelled bikes and weapons, 3D trees, horizon mountains, ground normal maps, the frame rate on the GT 1030; the owner asked for the exact plan first and then to go on)
- MEASURED FIRST (development tools in `_tmp/`, not shipped: `bench.js`, `prof.js`, `setup.js`; load them with fetch + eval after `Game.start({mode:'br'})`): the Browser pane at 1920x1080 (`resize_window`; at the pane's own small size everything is 3x faster and says nothing), the camera held at five places (densest forest, densest town, tall-grass meadow, foot of the mountain, the glide in the air), a quality tier at a time, `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` (it works in the pane) for the GPU time, rAF intervals, CPU time of `step` / `render`, draw calls and triangles from `renderer.info`; `Bench.prof(spot, tier, secs, [switches])` turns one thing off at a time and reports what it saved. The game is GPU bound (CPU under 8 ms a frame). The big items on the GT 1030 at High, in ms of a 37 ms frame: 4x MSAA on the half-float target 8-10, the leaf cards of the card trees 10 (their MeshStandardMaterial), the terrain 9, 16x anisotropy 5, shadows 8 (almost all of it is the lookup in the main pass, not the shadow map), grass 2.
- FRAME RATE, 1920 x 1080, this PC, no bots, frames per second old -> new: Low 54-60 -> 60, Medium 37-54 -> 54-60, High 26-33 -> 44-60 (forest 26 -> 46, town 27 -> 47, glide 33 -> 60), Ultra 21-25 -> 32-45. A real match with the default (auto) settings and 19 bots in the densest forest holds about 50 fps at High (auto starts at Ultra and steps down to High within 2 s, the resolution scale stays 1). What did it: (1) `src/engine/fastenv.js`, (2) FXAA, (3) anisotropy 8, (4) the terrain shader, (5) the modelled trees (cheaper than the old cards).
- FASTENV (`src/engine/fastenv.js`, loaded after sky.js): matte surfaces (rough, not metal: terrain, trees, grass, buildings, the horizon, props) use MeshLambertMaterial instead of MeshStandardMaterial and take the diffuse sky light from nine spherical-harmonics coefficients computed from the sky photo whenever the sky changes (`FastEnv.update()`, called at the end of `SkySys.makeEnv`: the photo is read the way `SKY_HDR_FRAG` does, turn / clamp / exposure / gain / ground) instead of the cube-UV environment lookups (about 16 texture reads a pixel). Done with `#ifdef FAST_ENV` additions to two three.js r158 shader chunks (`lights_pars_begin`, `lights_fragment_maps`; the install checks the revision and the chunk text and switches itself off on another three.js), `FastEnv.make(options)` (the options of a Standard material; returns Lambert + SH when matte, Standard otherwise), `FastEnv.hook` (intercepts `onBeforeCompile` with `Object.defineProperty` so a material may still get its own wind / terrain / grass code assigned afterwards; `customProgramCacheKey` carries the inner function's text), `FastEnv.convertTree(root)` (GLB props, called after `Assets.build`). Calibrated against the old look by comparing the mean colour of the finished picture at five places: `uG` 1.1 (ambient) and `gain` 1.04 (colour) bring the ratio to 0.95-0.99 (the first try was 7 % darker). Looks the same in the five times of day / themes that were looked at (day, golden, night, winter). Metals, glass, guns, characters, water keep the full model. `?fastenv=0` switches it off for comparing. Pitfall: the specular lobe a Lambert surface lacks is the difference left (about 3 % in the sun).
- ANTI-ALIASING: High uses FXAA (`FX.FXAAShader`, a pass after the output pass; `QUALITY.high.samples = 0, fxaa = true`), Ultra 4x MSAA (was 8x), Low / Medium unchanged (no post-processing). `Tex.aniso` is 8 (was 16).
- TERRAIN SHADER: a layer (grass / sand / rock / road) is only read where it has weight (about nine reads fewer on most of the ground; the derivatives are taken once outside the branches and handed to `textureGrad` through the `TG(...)` macro), snow and winter code behind branches. GROUND NORMAL MAPS: the four normal maps of the ground photographs (`assets/tex/*_n.jpg`, moved out of tools/unused-tex) are loaded by `Tex.loadPhotos()` after the photos (High / Ultra only), projected from above and added to the normal (UDN) within 24-72 m of the camera (`Terrain.uDetailN`, `Terrain.uNorm`); the albedo bump drops from 0.12 to 0.06 while they are on. Looked at: sand ripples and grit catch the sun now. The green channel is assumed OpenGL style (the relief looks right; not proven with a DirectX map).
- MODELLED TREES (`src/world/trees.js`, `assets/models/mk/`, Stylized Nature MegaKit Standard by Quaternius, CC0; `tools/make-trees.ps1` prepares them from the downloaded zip, which is only read: five pines, five broadleaf trees, 3.4 MB, the big PNGs became 1024 px, the bark lost its alpha test, the broadleaf trees get the pack's WHITE leaf picture so that each tree can be tinted: greens, a few autumn / golden / blossom ones): real trunks with branches, 3-6 k triangles. Three levels of detail made at load time (`Trees.thinCards` keeps 45 % / 16 % of the leaf cards and grows the rest by 1 / sqrt(keep); `Trees.cluster` merges vertices of a 0.12 / 0.5 m grid so thin branches vanish), every tree an instance of the level of its distance (38 / 105 / 520 m, 8 % hysteresis, re-sorted when the camera moved a metre or every 0.5 s), only levels 0 and 1 cast shadows, one instanced mesh per variant x part x level. The positions, scales and colliders are still those of `Nature.build` (the lists are `Nature.treeLists`; the variant comes from a hash of the position, no random numbers are used, so every player sees the same trees). High and Ultra only (`Trees.apply(key)`, called from `Game.applyQuality`; models load on first need, ~2.5 s); Low, Medium and phones keep the card trees (`Nature.cardTrees`, hidden while the modelled ones show; `Themes.mainVisible` goes through `Trees.shown` so the duel / training arenas hide the right ones). `?trees=0` shows the cards on any tier. The forest got faster, not slower (fewer fragments than the card trees). Dead desert trees (DeadTree_1-4 of the pack) were left out on purpose: their colliders would exist on Low / Medium without being drawn.
- HORIZON MOUNTAINS (`Terrain.horizon`): a denser mesh (480 x 30), craggy ridges (`crag`, `crinkle` noise) on top of the broad ones, forest -> rock from about 80 m -> snow only above about 250 m, a shader that breaks the colour up with the rock / grass textures (more rock where steep) and a third of the fog of the near world so the range stays a range. Before it was all white blobs.
- WEAPONS (`src/engine/models.js`): no CC0 minigun exists; the only CC0 RPG found (OpenGameArt "Low Poly Rocket Launcher", 1.2 MB, downloaded and looked at) is 1,236 triangles of chunky flat shapes in a Godot-only format, worse than what the code can make, so the minigun, the RPG and the Phantom-X are rebuilt from parts with new primitives in `GM.assemble`: `'r'` rounded box (`GM.roundBox`), `'l'` lathe around the z axis (a polyline out and back makes a thin shell: the RPG's flared rear, the rocket's ogive, the emitter), `'t'` torus ring, cylinders with 16 sides, a `brass` material. The minigun has a rotor of six barrels, hub and three clamps (`specs.minigun.spin`, still the `spin` group that turns while firing), gear housing, spade grips, carry handle, ammunition box and a belt of brass links; the RPG a tube with flared rear, wooden heat shield with straps, the loaded rocket (black nose cap, olive ogive, ring in the rarity colour), pistol grip with trigger guard, fore grip, side optic, rail; the Phantom-X a rounded white shell with cyan light strips, ringed barrel with an emitter cone, a glowing cell in the magazine well, a holographic ring. About 3.5 k triangles each. Muzzle, eject, sights and ADS positions unchanged. Looked at side-on and in first person (viewmodel) at all three.
- BIKES: the four bikes are still built from parts (no CC0 motorcycle of a decent quality exists: Kenney, Quaternius and Poly Haven have none, the free ones elsewhere are retro "PSX" bikes with a few hundred triangles). The GLB slot is built and tested: `assets/models/bikes/bikes.json` (ships empty) maps a bike id (scooter, sport, raider, hyper) to `{ file, length, yaw, scale, seat }`; `Vehicles.preload` (called from `Assets.build`) loads them, `Bike.buildGlb` stands the model on the ground, turns / scales it, finds `wheel_f` / `wheel_r` / `fork` (or front wheel, rear wheel, handlebar) by node name, puts pivots at the wheel centres and the steering head so the wheels turn and the fork steers like the procedural ones, and uses `seat` for the rider (`Bike.seatPos`, `Bike.wheelRad` are new members used by the procedural bike too). Tested with a GLB exported from a procedural bike inside the page (GLTFExporter -> GLTFLoader.parse): wheels and fork found, steering and wheel angles applied, size and seat right. NOT tested with a real third-party model.
- Checked after all this: every quality tier renders with the right kind of tree and no error, a duel and the tutorial (arena hides the open world and its trees, the menu brings them back), a bike ride (mount, 120 steps with a hop, dismount), a phone viewport (card trees, no error), golden / night / winter looks, a real match with the default settings for 30 s with 19 bots.
- Pitfalls met: `Remove-Item` with a wildcard on the project path through a PowerShell variable is refused by the tool's guard (use `-LiteralPath` on one file at a time); `Bench.place()` only moves the camera on the next frame (wait before measuring or placing things at it); a gun viewer must not return `userData` (the muzzle flash sprite carries its picture as base64, thousands of characters); the pane keeps its viewport size and localStorage between page loads (clear the storage after tests: they leave quality, time of day, perspective and coins behind); the GPU timer queries need `renderer.info.autoReset = false` to count the draw calls of every pass.
- Not done: Google sign-in, the global table and ranked seasons, a TURN server, a real phone, a real two-machine online test, deploying anything, a modelled motorcycle, dead trees in the desert. Release 4.4.0 was built with `tools\release.ps1 -Version 4.4.0 -Zip` (not sent or uploaded).

## v4.1.0 changes (owner: "make version 4.1 and finish everything that is not done yet")
- VERSION `4.1.0` (`src/boot.js`; shown as "Last Squad Standing 4.1"), service-worker cache `lss-v4.1`.
- 3D GRASS (`src/world/grass3d.js`, global `Grass3D`, High and Ultra on desktop only; `?grass3d=0` switches it off): real blade geometry (tapered 3-segment blades, 5 per clump on High and 6 on Ultra) around the camera, ONE instanced draw call, no CPU work per frame. The instances sit on a grid of 0.42 - 0.45 m cells that follows the camera (`gl_InstanceID` -> the world cell with the same index mod N within N / 2 cells of the camera), the clump position, height, lean, colour and thinning with distance come from hashes of the world cell in the vertex shader, so a cell keeps its blades while the camera moves. One 1 m `RG16F` texture (`Grass3D.buildMaps`, 28 ms, 0.8 MB) holds the terrain height (red, taken from the rendered terrain mesh) and the "grass grows here" mask (green: terrain grass weight x not on the shore / not on snow x low-frequency patches x not within 0.7 m of a house, wall or floor collider, 0.45 m of a trunk or rock, or 1.2 m of a loot spot). Blades shrink to nothing towards the edge of the radius (no popping, no sub-pixel flicker), are widened with distance, thinned randomly and faded towards the ground's own colour; they bend away from the player's feet and sway with `Nature.uTime`. Hidden above 24 m over the ground (gliding), inside duel / training arenas and in winter. Material: `MeshStandardMaterial` + `onBeforeCompile` (own normal pointing up so both sides are lit alike, receives the sun's shadows, casts none).
